# Thrust allocation — DP capability Level 1

This document explains `allocate_thrust(...)` in `thruster_allocation.py`: how
the actuator forces that balance one environmental load are found, and how
that maps to DNV-ST-0111 (Edition December 2021) [2.4.4], [3.8.2] and [3.11.1].
Unlike the load and thrust formulas, **the standard does not prescribe the
method here**. The linear program below is our choice.

## 1. Background

What the standard requires (text read from PDF pages 16, 29 and 35):
- **[2.4.4]:** "there shall at the same time be a balance of forces and a balance of moments, i.e. including forces and moments generated by the actuators and forces and moments caused by environmental forces". The calculation starts at the lowest environment and steps up to the first limiting condition. That stepping is step 6; this module balances one load.
- **[3.11.1]:** "The DP control system shall calculate the necessary actuator forces, and their directions, to find the balance between the environmental forces and actuator forces."
  - Its guidance note says the allocation in the analysis may differ from the one in the vessel's real DP control system, and so may give different solutions.
  - Level 1 therefore fixes *what* must balance, not *how* the forces are shared.
- **[3.8.2]:** the actuator angle is 0° when the actuator pushes forward along x, and it increases anti-clockwise. So 90° pushes to port and 270° to starboard. This is the "Thr direction" of Table A-8.

The load is `environmental_loads_level1(hull, bf, direction_deg)`, already
multiplied by the dynamic factor 1.25. Each actuator can give at most its
effective thrust `T = effective_thrust(thruster)` ([3.9.1]); see `thrust.md`.

### Coordinate system

Positions and forces are in the [2.8.2] frame: x forward, y to port, yaw
positive counter-clockwise, origin at Lpp/2 on the centreline. A force
(fx, fy) at (x, y) gives the yaw moment `Mz = x·fy − y·fx`.

## 2. Formulas

### Balance ([2.4.4])

With `fx_i, fy_i` the force from actuator i at `(x_i, y_i)`, and `(Fx, Fy, Mz)`
the environmental load:

```
Σ fx_i                   = −Fx
Σ fy_i                   = −Fy
Σ (x_i·fy_i − y_i·fx_i)  = −Mz
```

### What each actuator can give

`T` is the forward effective thrust and `T_rev = effective_thrust(thruster, reverse=True)`.

| Kind | Force | Limit |
|---|---|---|
| azimuth, pod, cycloidal | any direction | \|f\| ≤ T, as an inscribed polygon (below) |
| tunnel | along y only (fx = 0) | −T_rev ≤ fy ≤ T (the same both ways, Table 3-2) |
| shaft line (no rudder) | along x only (fy = 0) | −T_rev ≤ fx ≤ T |

- An azimuthing actuator never needs reverse thrust, because it can turn round. It only uses T.
- Water jets are rejected by `effective_thrust` ([3.8.1]).

**The polygon.** A linear program cannot use the circle |f| ≤ T directly.
It is replaced by a regular polygon with N sides **inscribed** in the circle,
so it never promises more thrust than T:

```
cos φ_k · fx + sin φ_k · fy ≤ T · cos(π/N),     φ_k = (2k + 1)·π/N,   k = 0 … N−1
```

- The corners lie at 0°, 360°/N, 2·360°/N, …
- With the default N = 36 there is a corner every 10°, including 0°, 90°, 180° and 270°. So pure surge and pure sway get the full T.
- Halfway between two corners the polygon reaches `T·cos(π/N)`. That is the largest loss: `cos 5° = 0.9962`, i.e. 0.4%.

### Utilisation and the two linear programs

Each actuator's limit is scaled by one number u, the **utilisation**: the
highest force any actuator needs, as a fraction of its effective thrust.

1. **Pass 1:** minimise u, subject to the balance and every force lying inside u × its limit. The load is balanced if **u ≤ 1**.
   - This is the same problem as "maximise λ such that the thrusters give λ·(−load)": u = 1/λ, because each capacity set is convex and contains 0.
   - Written with u, zero load (BF 0) gives u = 0 rather than an unbounded problem.
   - If no amount of thrust can give the load (e.g. tunnels only against a surge load), the problem is infeasible. Then u = ∞ and the forces are `nan`.
2. **Pass 2:** keep u (plus a slack of `TOLERANCE` = 10⁻⁶) and minimise the total thrust `Σ |f_i|`.
   - For azimuths |f_i| is measured with the polygon: `t_i ≥ (cos φ_k·fx_i + sin φ_k·fy_i) / cos(π/N)` for every k. This equals |f_i| at the corners.
   - For tunnels and shaft lines it is `t_i ≥ ±f_i`.
   - Why it's needed: pass 1 only fixes the most loaded actuator. Actuators with room to spare could push against each other at no cost, and their forces and angles would be meaningless in Table A-8.

u is reported for the whole thruster set. When it is not exactly 0 or ∞,
at least one actuator is at u × its limit.

Inside the LP the forces are divided by the largest limit, so the numbers are
of order 1. The moment row then holds the lever arms in m.

## 3. Checking the signs

- **Head-on load** (Fx < 0, pushed aft): the azimuths push forward, fx > 0, angle 0°.
- **Load from starboard** (Fy > 0, pushed to port): the actuators push to starboard, fy < 0, angle 270°.
- **Counter-clockwise load moment** (Mz > 0, bow to port): the actuators must turn the bow to starboard.
  - A bow tunnel (x > 0) pushes to starboard: `x·fy < 0`.
  - A port azimuth (y > 0) pushes forward: `−y·fx < 0`.
  - A starboard azimuth (y < 0) pushes aft: `−y·fx < 0`.
- **Port/starboard mirror:** the test vessel's utilisation at 270° equals that at 90° (both 0.5874 at BF 6).

The tests in `tests/models/test_thruster_allocation.py` check each of these with
round loads written as multiples of T.

### Worked example (the test vessel, `config.THRUSTERS`)

BF 6, environment from 90° (starboard beam). The factored load from HANDOVER §4
is (Fx, Fy, Mz) = (−5.5 kN, 358.0 kN, 509.0 kNm). `allocate_thrust` gives
**u = 0.5874**, so the load is balanced:

| Thruster | T [kN] | fx [kN] | fy [kN] | \|f\| [kN] | Angle [°] | \|f\| / T |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| AZ1 (−40, +5.5) | 339.77 | 183.05 | −77.90 | 198.94 | 336.95 | 0.5855 |
| AZ2 (−40, −5.5) | 339.77 | −177.51 | −89.80 | 198.93 | 206.83 | 0.5855 |
| BT1 (+31, 0) | 122.19 | 0 | −71.78 | 71.78 | 270 | 0.5874 |
| BT2 (+28, 0) | 114.20 | 0 | −67.08 | 67.08 | 270 | 0.5874 |
| RAZ (+22, 0) | 87.48 | 0 | −51.39 | 51.39 | 270 | 0.5874 |

- The three forward actuators all push straight to starboard at exactly u.
- The aft azimuths show 0.5855 < u. They lie on a side of the polygon (between the corners at 330° and 340° for AZ1), where the polygon is inside the circle. So measured against the polygon they are at u, and |f|/T is slightly lower.
- The aft azimuths **push against each other in surge**: AZ1 forward, AZ2 aft.
  - This is useful work, not waste. The load moment of +509 kNm must be met by −509 kNm.
  - Pushing to starboard at x = −40 turns the bow the *wrong* way (+). The azimuths' opposite surge forces at y = ±5.5 give a clockwise couple instead.
  - Their net fx = 183.05 − 177.51 = 5.54 kN balances Fx.

Check by hand:
- Fx: 183.05 − 177.51 = 5.54 ✓.
- Fy: −77.90 − 89.80 − 71.78 − 67.08 − 51.39 = −357.95 ✓.
- Mz: (−40)(−77.90) + (−40)(−89.80) + 31(−71.78) + 28(−67.08) + 22(−51.39) − 5.5·183.05 − (−5.5)(−177.51) = 3116.0 + 3592.0 − 2225.2 − 1878.2 − 1130.6 − 1006.8 − 976.3 = −509.1 ✓.

**Beam capability of the test vessel.** With the same approach:
- BF 7 gives u = 0.826;
- BF 8 (Fy = 664 kN, Mz = 1368 kNm) gives u = 1.106.

So the test vessel is BF 7 in beam seas. HANDOVER's first estimate of "roughly
BF 8" added up all sway thrust (≈ 1000 kN), but the moment balance does not
allow that. The forward group (tunnels + RAZ, 324 kN at x ≈ 27.5 m) has to
match the moment of the aft azimuths at x = −40 m. Even a pure 664 kN sway load
with no moment gives u = 1.055.

## 4. In the code

- `allocate_thrust(thrusters, load, n_sides=36)` in `dp_capability/models/thruster_allocation.py`:
  - `load` is `(Fx, Fy, Mz)` for **one** heading, e.g. one element of `environmental_loads_level1(...)`. It is not vectorized: step 6 calls it once per heading and BF (432 calls take about 1 s).
  - `n_sides` is the polygon's number of sides. It is a numerical choice, not a value from the standard, so it is a keyword argument and not in `standard.py`.
- It returns an `Allocation` (frozen dataclass):
  - `fx`, `fy`: force per actuator [N], in the order of `thrusters`;
  - `utilisation`: u from pass 1 (∞ when the load can't be given);
  - `feasible`: `utilisation ≤ 1 + TOLERANCE`;
  - `angle_deg`: the [3.8.2] direction per actuator, `nan` for an idle one.
- `_capacity_rows()` builds the limit rows per actuator kind (the table in §2). `_solve()` wraps `scipy.optimize.linprog` (HiGHS).
- Not included yet (step 7): ventilation, flushing and skeg losses, forbidden zones, rudders and power limits. Forbidden zones make an azimuth's capacity set non-convex; see HANDOVER §6.
